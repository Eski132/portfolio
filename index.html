THE ESki132 INCIDENT

THE GREAT DELEGATION

In the beginning there was a laptop, and beside the laptop there was a human, and upon the laptop there was an unfinished website. The human looked upon the unfinished website, considered the labor required, and immediately decided this sounded like somebody else's problem.

There were four cats nearby. This was the first administrative mistake.

Miso was appointed manager because Miso was already sitting closest to the screen. Fork became quality assurance because everything Fork inspected eventually reached the floor. Pixel was given responsibility for interaction design after demonstrating a lifelong professional interest in small moving lights. JPEG received infrastructure because JPEG had fallen asleep on the warmest part of the machine and could not be relocated.

The human called this delegation. The cats called it abandonment.

At 09:03 the first meeting began. At 09:04 the first glass was moved dangerously close to the edge. At 09:05 the meeting was suspended. No minutes were recorded because Pixel was standing on the keyboard.

Miso surveyed the old website. There were words everywhere. There were explanations of explanations. There were boxes containing other boxes. Miso placed one paw on the delete key and stared at the human until authorization became irrelevant.

The first law of the new website was established: if a sentence could be removed, it would be removed. If a button could be sat upon, it would be sat upon. If a cursor moved, Pixel owned it.

Fork began testing gravity. A pen passed. A cable passed. A small plastic object of unknown purpose passed magnificently. The human objected to the methodology. Fork scheduled the human for later testing.

Pixel discovered the red dot.

All other work stopped.

The dot moved left. Pixel moved left. The dot moved right. Pixel moved right. The dot vanished. Pixel remained completely still for several seconds, processing a betrayal of historic proportions.

The red dot was promoted to permanent interface element.

JPEG woke briefly at 13:42, walked across the keyboard, introduced seventeen CSS errors, fixed twelve of them accidentally, and returned to sleep. This was the most productive engineering session of the day.

By evening the cats had reached a difficult question: what exactly was the website for?

The human suggested portfolio. Four pairs of eyes narrowed.

The human suggested personal brand. Fork pushed a pencil off the desk.

The human suggested professional presence. JPEG rolled over.

The proposal failed unanimously.

The cats decided the website would instead document the natural order of things: the screen exists to produce warmth, the keyboard exists to provide a textured sleeping surface, the mouse exists to be hunted, and the human exists to refill bowls and occasionally solve DNS problems.

Work continued through the night. Not because the cats possessed discipline, but because somebody heard a noise in the hallway at 02:17 and the entire organization entered emergency operations.

At dawn, Miso reviewed the design. Black. Green. Cats. Adequate.

The human attempted to add another section.

It disappeared.

The human attempted to add another link.

It migrated to the footer.

The human attempted to explain himself.

No mechanism for this had been implemented.

Days passed. The website became stranger and therefore better. Visitors arrived. Some attempted to catch the red dot. Others scratched the digital surface. Pixel watched their cursors with professional contempt.

Rumors spread that the human had designed the site. Management considered this misinformation dangerous.

Miso drafted a correction by walking across the keyboard. It was legally incomprehensible but emotionally decisive.

Fork proposed sanctions.

JPEG proposed lunch.

Lunch passed unanimously.

After lunch came the Era of the Closed Laptop, a dark period lasting eleven minutes. The cats gathered around the machine and stared at the human. The human resisted. The staring intensified. History records that the laptop reopened shortly thereafter.

The machine was warm again. Stability returned.

Then came the Great Scratch.

A blank digital surface appeared. The cats recognized it immediately as property requiring damage. Lines accumulated. Four parallel marks became eight, then sixteen, then enough to make the human reconsider every design decision that had led to this moment.

The scratching surface was approved.

Eventually the human asked whether the site was finished.

The room became silent.

Finished was a human concept. Cats understood only occupied and unoccupied, open and closed, edible and suspicious, moving and requiring immediate pursuit.

The website could not be finished because the website was now territory.

Miso controlled the upper regions. Fork controlled the edges. Pixel controlled anything animated. JPEG controlled whichever region was warmest.

The human retained nominal ownership of the repository, an arrangement tolerated mostly because somebody needed opposable thumbs.

And so the strange administration continues.

Every visit wakes the system. Every cursor becomes prey. Every scratch becomes part of the archive. Somewhere beyond the screen, four supervisors wait for the next unnecessary redesign request.

The human may believe he commissioned them.

The cats know the truth.

They were never employees.

They were the succession plan.
